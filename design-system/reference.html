0.01How to read this

Three kinds of rule, and only one of them is brand law

The brand guidelines cover color, typography, and logo. They say nothing about buttons, shadows, corner radii, spacing, or layout — so a large part of any usable design system has to be designed, not transcribed. Rather than blur that line, every rule here carries its source.

BRAND

Stated in the guidelines. Don't change it without brand approval.

SITE

Measured from production Sunday Swagger page markup.

SYSTEM

Designed here to fill a gap. Overrule it freely — then write down your call.

The gap you should know about. sundayswagger.com is blocked by this environment's network egress policy, so the live storefront could not be crawled. Buttons, shadows, radii and spacing below are SYSTEM — reasoned from the brand's stated aesthetic, not copied off production. Section 0.11 lists four ways to close that in about two minutes.

0.02Foundation

One sentence governs every decision below

“Keep a clean, modern, and sleek look, while allowing our loud designs to speak for themselves.”

That is the entire system compressed. The prints on the product are the loud part. The frame around them — layout, buttons, type, chrome — stays black and white and gets out of the way. When a composition feels wrong, the fix is almost always to take color out.

0.03Color

Black and white are the brand. Everything else is an accent.

Six values, and a strict hierarchy between them. Grey is a background, never type. Blue, green and pink are — in the guidelines' own words — for “lines or accents on specific design elements or words in copy.” One accent per composition.

Print swatches don't match the spec'd hex. The chips printed in the guidelines are the CMYK builds converted back to RGB: blue renders #1CBFD5, green #B0D235, pink #EC048C. Green is the widest gap — the CMYK build is a much duller olive-lime than the electric #C2FC08, so if the electric green matters on a printed piece, ask for a spot or extended-gamut match.

The five rules that fall out of it

  1. Never white text on blue, green or pink. Green-on-white is effectively invisible at 1.22:1.
  2. Accent-filled buttons take black labels. All three pass comfortably that way.
  3. Blue and green work as text only on black. On white they're underlines, rules, dots, and fills behind black type.
  4. Pink is the one accent that reads as type on white — 18px bold or 24px regular and up. Below that, black.
  5. Grey #ECECEC is never a text color. For muted copy use #6B6D70 (5.9:1 on white).

0.10Email & adsSYSTEM

Gmail will not load your display face. Plan for it.

Gmail, Outlook desktop and Yahoo all strip webfonts, so Futura PT Condensed and Tilt Warp won't render for most of your list. If a display headline is doing brand work — a drop announcement, a sale number, the hero — export it as an image with real alt text and a background color behind it, so it still reads when images are off. Everything else stays live text.

Email frame

  • 600px body, single column, table layout, inline styles, bulletproof VML buttons.
  • Outer canvas #ECECEC or #000000; content #FFFFFF or #000000.
  • Body copy never below 16px, fine print never below 12px, tap targets ≥48px.
  • Many clients auto-invert for dark mode — set #000000 explicitly rather than relying on transparency, and ship the white logo PNG for dark sections.
  • One accent per send. Not five.

0.11What's still open

Close the live-site gap and half of this stops being a proposal

Two of the three uses — website edits and ads that match the site — depend on matching the storefront exactly, and this environment can't reach sundayswagger.com. Any one of these fixes it, fastest first:

  1. Paste the theme CSS. Shopify admin → Online Store → Themes → Edit code → assets/*.css. Even just the :root block and the .button rules is enough.
  2. View-source a product page and paste the <head> — that gives the font loads and CSS variables in one shot.
  3. Have your admin allow the domain for this session's egress policy, and it can be crawled directly.
  4. Screenshots of the homepage, a PDP and the cart drawer — enough to confirm radius, shadow and button treatment by eye.

With any of those, every SYSTEM tag in sections 0.07–0.09 becomes SITE, and this stops being a well-reasoned proposal and starts being a mirror of what's actually shipping.

Two decisions waiting on you

  • Lato or Work Sans for body copy? The brand book says Lato; production renders Work Sans. Pick one and the other gets retired.
  • Is the electric green worth a spot color in print? The CMYK build lands materially duller than #C2FC08. If that green is load-bearing on printed pieces, it needs a spot or extended-gamut match.
